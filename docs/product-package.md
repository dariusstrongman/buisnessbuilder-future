# The Company Build Room

## Decision memo

**Thesis.** Do not sell “AI makes a company.” Sell a controlled assembly service that turns Billy Bob’s lawn-care intent into owned, inspected operating assets. The product’s hero is the build room: every recommendation, approval, cost, action, test, and piece of evidence is visible. AI is labor under policy, not the product promise.

**Recommended v1.** Texas-based, owner-operated mobile services with one founder, one service area, simple quoting, no employees, no regulated work, and no custom software. Start with lawn maintenance, residential cleaning, mobile detailing, pressure washing, and pet-waste removal. Deliver a website, brand starter, service design, lead intake, quoting and scheduling workflow, customer communications, account map, compliance checklist, launch tests, and handoff.

**Do not build yet.** Autonomous filings; custody of founder credentials or funds; regulated professional advice; payroll; tax filing; insurance brokerage; ad-spend automation; multi-location/franchise operations; marketplace supply; custom apps; unbounded AI agents; guaranteed “10/10” creative scores; or a universal industry ontology.

**Key risks.** The website engine has not completed a real paid journey; concierge labor may erase margin; “business builder” can be mistaken for legal advice; local requirements vary; external accounts break automated verification; weak source data can create confident but false readiness; and Run My Business can become an agency with software margins.

**Pricing tests.** Test three transparent setup anchors: Website $995/$1,495; Business $2,495/$3,995; Run $499/$899 monthly after a $1,500–$2,500 operating setup. Keep every vendor/state fee as founder-paid pass-through. Measure close rate, completion time, gross labor hours, revision count, attach rate, and 30-day support burden before declaring prices.

**Next 2–4 weeks.** Week 1: five founder interviews and two lawn-care shadow sessions; freeze intake and readiness rubric. Week 2: manually fulfill one no-charge design partner through the build room, logging every minute and blocker. Week 3: fulfill two paid pilots, test both price anchors, and perform independent website/workflow QA. Week 4: tighten supported scope, publish evidence-backed samples, and decide whether Run My Business remains concierge or becomes a narrower Inbox + Follow-up Operator.

## 1. End-to-end Billy Bob journey

1. **Landing and offer.** Billy sees three concrete outcomes: “A website customers can trust,” “A business assembled around how you actually work,” and “Ongoing help operating approved workflows.” Each shows exclusions, estimated founder time, pass-through costs, and ownership.
2. **Founder intake.** Conversational intake asks where Billy works, current customers, experience, equipment, vehicle, weekly availability, service radius, budget, scheduling preferences, existing photos/reviews, and jobs he will not take. Unknown answers remain unknown.
3. **Research.** The system assembles a cited local brief: demand signals, visible competitors, typical offer patterns, seasonal/weather considerations, license triggers, and customer expectations. Facts, estimates, and inferences are labeled.
4. **Challenge and recommendation.** Instead of accepting “full-service landscaping,” it challenges scope. Recommendation: begin with recurring mow/edge/blow inside 12 miles; exclude pesticide, irrigation, tree, and hardscape work until independently cleared and equipped.
5. **Approval.** Billy approves, revises, or rejects the wedge, service radius, starting-price hypothesis, capacity assumption, and exclusions. No build starts from silent defaults.
6. **Name, domain, brand.** Three names receive conflict, domain, pronunciation, and trust notes. Availability is a time-stamped observation, not a reservation. Billy approves the name; Billy purchases the domain in his own registrar account. Brand concepts are proposed and separately approved.
7. **Blueprint.** One-page operating model: customer, problem, services, exclusions, territory, acquisition, intake, quote rules, schedule, capacity, service standards, payment policy, complaint path, metrics, risks, and 30-day tests.
8. **Formation/admin.** Every item offers **Do It For Me / Guide Me / Skip**. “Do it” means prepare and coordinate within permitted authority; signatures, attestations, identity checks, professional advice, and payments remain founder-only. Skipped items keep visible consequences and may block Fully Set.
9. **Live assembly.** The build room streams discrete work cards with owner, dependency, cost, status, evidence, and rollback. It never uses fake activity text.
10. **Website/media/AI enhancement.** Build the site from Billy’s real service area, equipment, photos, policies, and voice. Synthetic media is disclosed and cannot masquerade as completed work or customers. AI drafts, classifies, and recommends under explicit permissions.
11. **Infrastructure.** Billy owns domain, email, website account, analytics, CRM/field-service account, phone number, payment account, media, and exports. Builder access is delegated and revocable.
12. **AI workforce.** V1 roles: Intake Assistant, Quote Drafting Assistant, Inbox Assistant, and Review Follow-up Assistant. Each has an objective, tools, budget, denied actions, escalation policy, and audit log.
13. **Founder-only actions.** Buy domain, accept provider terms, identity verification, banking/payment setup, sign/submit filings, choose insurance, attest licenses, approve public claims, approve exceptional quotes/refunds, hire workers, and authorize spend.
14. **Verification.** Every asset moves **Proposed → Executed → Tested → Verified**. A screenshot proves appearance, not function. A test proves a scenario, not legal compliance. External clearance is recorded only from the relevant authority/provider.
15. **Ready vs Fully Set.** Ready means Billy can safely accept the defined first customer through a tested path, with blockers disclosed. Fully Set means all selected launch requirements and external/founder actions are verified, monitoring is active, and no critical waiver remains.
16. **Handoff.** **Take the Keys** exports assets, account map, credentials checklist, policies, evidence, and runbook. **Run It For Me** grants revocable, scoped access with budgets and approval thresholds. Cancellation never transfers Billy’s ownership back to the builder.
17. **Operate/grow/expand.** Weekly operating brief shows leads, response time, quote acceptance, booked capacity, completion, exceptions, reviews, cost, and agent actions. Expansion proposals require evidence and founder approval before new territory, service, spend, or staffing.

## 3. Company Brain v1

The Company Brain is an evidence-backed operating graph, not a long prompt. Core entities: Company, Party, Offer, Decision, Market, Service, Policy, Capacity Model, Asset, Account, Vendor, Workflow, Agent, Task, Approval, Verification, Evidence, Metric, Risk, Obligation, and Audit Event.

Relationships are explicit: Company **owned by** Party; Service **available in** Market; Workflow **implements** Policy; Task **depends on** Decision/Asset; Verification **evaluates** Asset/Workflow/Account; Evidence **supports** Verification; Agent **may act on** Workflow under Permission; Obligation **arises from** Jurisdiction/Choice.

Every material record stores source/provenance, captured time, confidence, state, owner, permissions, version, and supersession. Confidence never upgrades a record to Verified. Evidence is content-addressed; audit events are append-only. PII and secrets are references to segregated stores, never copied into the general graph. The machine-readable populated example is in `company-brain-v1.json`.

Lifecycle: draft → challenged → approved → assembly → ready → fully_set → operating → paused → archived. Changes to scope, jurisdiction, ownership, provider, policy, or workflow invalidate dependent verifications.

| Professional service | above + engagement, deliverable, confidentiality, conflict check | credentials, jurisdiction, client fit, scope, turnaround, review | licensed-title/practice restrictions, privilege/confidentiality, professional liability, advice disclaimers | conflict/fit screen, signed scope, secure exchange, review and delivery pass |

Shared readiness primitives are ownership, truthful public claims, reachable contact path, defined service and exclusions, price/quote rule, capacity, schedule, payment/refund policy, privacy/terms, support path, test evidence, incident/rollback, and location-specific obligation review.

## 5. Verification catalog

| Critical target | Proposed | Executed | Tested | Verified evidence | Owner / expiry and failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name/domain | candidates + checks | founder purchased | DNS resolves in test | registrar receipt + founder-owned account + production DNS | External/founder; reverify on transfer/DNS change. Collision blocks launch. |
| Brand/public claims | concept | files rendered | contrast, legibility, claim review | founder approval + source-backed claims + asset export | Human/founder; reverify on substantive copy change. |
| Website | approved spec | deployed preview | mobile, links, forms, performance/accessibility smoke tests | production URL, test bundle, independent QA, owner access | Automated + human; reverify each deploy. Roll back failed release. |
| Lead form | field/consent spec | connected | valid, invalid, spam, duplicate, out-of-radius fixtures | stored test records + notifications + deletion test | Automated; 30–90 days and on change. Disable intake on data loss. |
| Email/phone | provider proposal | account configured | inbound/outbound, SPF/DKIM/DMARC where relevant, voicemail | founder-owned account, delivery evidence, recovery path | Provider + automated + founder; periodic. Failed sends queue and alert. |
| Quote workflow | policy | workflow enabled | standard, exception, tax, radius, capacity cases | expected outputs, founder threshold proof, audit log | Automated + human; on price/policy change. No quote if required field missing. |
| Scheduling | rules | calendar connected | conflicts, buffers, timezone, cancel/reschedule | booked test, calendar receipt, capacity invariants | Automated + founder; on calendar/rules change. Stop booking on sync failure. |
| Payment | chosen provider | founder opens account | test-mode success/failure/refund | provider verification + founder attestation + live-mode micro-test only if founder authorizes | External/founder; continuous monitoring. Never store raw card data. |
| Formation | selected path | documents prepared/submitted by authorized actor | field completeness only | authority-issued confirmation/status | Founder/external; status rechecked before claims. Preparation is not formation. |
| License/permit | obligation identified | application/record supplied | scope mapped to authorization | current issuing-authority record | External; expiry-based. Exclude service if unclear/expired. |
| Insurance | coverage need identified | quote/policy selected by founder | named insured/activity/date checked | policy declaration/certificate + founder confirmation | External/founder; expiry. No “insured” claim after lapse. |
| AI agent | objective/policy/tools | sandbox config | adversarial, exception, budget, permission tests | signed policy version + passing suite + monitored pilot | Automated + human; on model/tool/policy change and every 90 days. Pause on breach. |
| Handoff/export | manifest | export generated | restore/open checks | founder receipt + checksum + access revocation test | Founder/human; at handoff/cancellation. Retry before access removal. |

**State contract.** Proposed means designed but not acted. Executed means a real artifact/configuration exists. Tested means predefined scenarios passed in the stated environment. Verified means required evidence, owner, scope, freshness, and independent or external checks are all satisfied. Failure returns the item to the highest still-supported lower state, creates an incident, blocks dependents, and requires a new test after correction. Evidence expiration or dependency change removes Verified automatically.

**Ready:** the selected offer can accept and serve one customer safely within a narrow, disclosed scope; website/contact/intake/quote/schedule/support are tested; ownership and rollback exist; no critical blocker is open. **Fully Set:** Ready plus every selected formation/admin/provider/compliance task is externally or founder verified, production monitoring works, handoff is complete, and no material waiver remains. A company may launch Ready while explicitly not Fully Set only when the remaining items are noncritical and Billy accepts them.

## 6. Offer and pricing hypotheses

| Offer | Setup hypothesis | Recurring | Includes | Excludes / pass-through |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Build My Website | test $995 vs $1,495 | optional $79 care | strategy, 5-page site, copy, basic brand alignment, lead form, analytics, QA, handoff | domain, email, premium software, photography, ad spend; no formation or operations |
| Build My Business | test $2,495 vs $3,995 | optional $149 care | website plus research, challenge, blueprint, brand starter, account map, admin guidance, CRM/FSM setup, tested lead-to-book path, launch runbook | filing fees, insurance, licenses, equipment, subscriptions, legal/tax advice; one archetype/location |
| Run My Business | $1,500–$2,500 operating setup | test $499 vs $899/mo | approved inbox/intake/follow-up workflows, weekly brief, monitoring, bounded improvements, support window | fulfillment labor, sales guarantees, ad spend, bookkeeping/tax, payroll, 24/7 phone answering unless separately sourced |

Upsells: on-site photo session, additional service area, additional appointment resource, review migration, advanced local SEO, call answering partner, CRM/FSM migration, and quarterly operating review. Each needs an actual cost/labor model.

Cancellation: Billy retains every account, domain, dataset, creative asset, configuration export, and customer record he owns. Revoke builder access, deliver a readable export and dependency list, disclose which third-party subscriptions continue, and never hold a domain hostage. Unused prepaid service follows contract terms; pass-through costs already incurred are not treated as builder revenue.

Benchmarks are context, not value proof: current official pages show Wix self-serve plans beginning around $17/month and Jobber from $29/month on annual billing, while Housecall Pro advertises plans from $59/month. Texas LLC filing is $300; the IRS EIN is free. These demonstrate that the offer cannot compete as mere tool access. It must justify itself through assembly, judgment, evidence, and handoff.

## 7. First 10 customers

**ICP.** Texas owner-operators already capable of delivering a service but held back by fragmented setup: 0–3 workers, 1–30 current customers, one service area, under five core services, responsive founder, real equipment/experience, and willingness to use founder-owned accounts. Not “idea only” founders with no delivery ability.

**Support first:** lawn maintenance without chemical/irrigation/tree work; residential cleaning; mobile detailing; pressure washing with a disposal/municipal check; pet-waste removal. **Defer:** salons/barbers, tutoring, photography, bookkeeping, home inspection, handyman, pool service, and food due to appointment complexity, professional/licensing ambiguity, minors/privacy, trade scope, chemicals, or health rules. **Refuse initially:** legal/medical/financial advice, childcare, security, transport, construction/trades requiring licenses, pesticide application, weapons, controlled substances, adult content, deceptive lead generation, and any founder who will not own or verify accounts.

**Acquisition.** Founder-led local outreach to operators with weak/no site; referrals from equipment dealers and local business communities; before/after teardown content; and five paid discovery slots. No mass cold automation in v1.

**Concierge flow.** 30-minute qualification → paid fixed-scope pilot → evidence intake → blueprint/recommendation → approval → five-day assembly target → founder-only action session → verification day → handoff → day 7/14/30 check-ins. Cap support to one channel, business hours, two structured revision rounds, one archetype/location, and named deliverables.

**Discovery questions.** What work do you already perform well? What do customers ask for repeatedly? Where will you travel? What will you never do? What equipment and insurance do you have? How many jobs fit a week after travel/weather? How do leads arrive and how fast can you answer? What requires an on-site quote? Which claims can you prove? Who controls current accounts? What would make you refuse a job? What must you personally approve?

**Instrumentation.** Funnel conversion; intake completion; time to recommendation; approval latency; founder action latency; build labor by asset; rework; pass/fail by verification; time to Ready/Fully Set; vendor cost; support contacts; lead delivery success; response time; quote/book flow completion; founder intervention; export success; cancellation/access-revocation success. Track customer business metrics as observations, not promises.

**Success criteria.** At least 8/10 reach Ready; 7/10 reach Fully Set or have only explicit noncritical deferrals; zero ownership or permission incidents; every public claim sourced/approved; 100% export test pass; median founder time under three hours after intake; fulfillment labor supports the tested price; and at least 6/10 would pay the same amount again. Stop adding customers after any credential, payment, public-claim, or compliance incident until reviewed.

**Feedback cadence.** End of each approval, launch day, day 7, day 14, day 30. Ask what felt unclear, unsafe, slow, generic, or unnecessary; which evidence created trust; and what Billy still had to do manually.

## Assumptions and unresolved founder decisions

Assumptions: Texas is the first operating jurisdiction; the first Billy Bob is an existing capable operator, not a novice; concierge service is acceptable while learning; user-provided facts are not treated as verified; no live filing or purchasing occurs; and Website/Business offers can be sold without Run.

Founder decisions still required: product/brand name; whether $995 remains the website anchor; target gross margin and maximum concierge hours; whether formation coordination is included or referred out; exact support hours; whether Ready can launch with noncritical skips; which CRM/FSM provider is the default; whether Run begins with inbox/follow-up only; data retention; AI disclosure standard; refund/revision policy; and which Texas metro hosts the first cohort.
